### Features

- **Session trace**: every recording reports once a second what it costs and whether all of it arrives — sample rate, ingest lag, breaks in the time stamps (dropped samples), pipeline CPU, bytes written, failed writes, epochs sent to and dropped by the embedder, daemon CPU and peak memory. Sent as a `SessionTrace` event, logged once a minute (at once when storage fails), and kept under `trace` in the recording's sidecar JSON.
- **Parquet recordings survive a crash**: after every row group the raw EEG file is synced and its footer checkpointed (`.parquet.ckpt`), and rows not yet in a row group go to a small journal (`.parquet.tail`). A recording left without its footer by a crash, a kill or a power cut is rebuilt the next time a recording starts. Both side files are removed on a clean close.
- **LSL streams are stamped on this machine's clock**: EEG samples and markers are mapped to this computer's Unix time through LSL clock synchronisation (the outlet's time service, re-measured every few seconds), so a source on another computer — whose wall clock may differ from ours — is recorded on the same timeline as markers and labels. When the time service does not answer, a stream's declared wall-clock reference (`<clock_reference>`, as the `nesdk-lsl` bridge writes) is used, else its arrival time. Previously LSL recordings carried the sender's process clock.
- **Automatic recording format**: new `auto` storage format, now the default for new installs: CSV for small streams, Parquet once a stream reaches 4 096 values per second (channels × sample rate), e.g. 16 channels at 256 Hz. Existing settings are kept as they are.
- **LSL markers**: during an LSL session every marker stream on the network (type `Markers`, text or numeric, including ones that start later) is followed. The network is searched for new marker streams about once a second: a stream that appears during a session is joined within about 1.5 s, and what it sends before that is lost (LSL does not replay). Each marker is written to the recording's new `_markers.csv` sidecar with its sub-second Unix time, broadcast as a `Marker` event, exported to BIDS as a `marker` event, and stored as a label when it differs from the previous marker of its stream (a repeated trigger becomes one label a minute, not one per repetition).
- **Lossless recording (setting, off by default)**: Settings → Recording Format → *Lossless recording*, or `POST /v1/settings/lossless-recording`. Samples, markers and sensor data that arrive while the recording cannot be opened (disk full, directory missing) are held in memory — up to 128 MB, a quarter of an hour of 32 channels at 500 Hz — and written in order once it opens; and when a session is cancelled, what the device has already delivered is written before the session closes instead of being discarded. Data written late is recorded but not analysed.
- **Integer sources are recorded as integers**: an LSL stream of integers (`int8`…`int64`, e.g. `nesdk-lsl --int`: the amplifier's own nanovolt counts) is stored in the Parquet recording as `int64` counts with delta encoding, with the microvolts one count stands for in the file metadata (`skill.eeg_scale_uv`). Exact, and less than half the size: an hour of a 32-channel 500 Hz amplifier is 163 MB instead of 357 MB as `f64`. Readers (`read_eeg_parquet`, BIDS export, re-embedding) get microvolts as before; crash recovery keeps the form. Other adapters can opt in through `DeviceAdapter::eeg_scale_uv`.
- **LSL units are honoured**: a stream that declares its channels in nanovolts, millivolts or volts is converted to the microvolts the pipeline works in (it used to be taken as microvolts whatever it said). Streams without a unit, or with one that is not a voltage, are unchanged.
- **Clock check while recording (setting, on by default)**: every five minutes during a session this computer's clock is compared with a time server (SNTP; by default the server the operating system's own clock service uses — `time.apple.com`, `time.windows.com`, `pool.ntp.org`). The difference is never applied to the clock or to the time stamps: it is reported (`ClockCheck` event, `clock_offset_ms` / `clock_uncertainty_ms` in the session trace), kept beside the recording in `exg_<ts>_clock.csv`, and a clock more than 50 ms out raises a warning. `skill_data::wall_clock::ClockCorrection` fits a recording's checks (offset and drift, weighted by network round trip) to correct time stamps afterwards, and the BIDS export states the measured offset in the EEG sidecar (`ClockOffsetSeconds`, `ClockDriftPPM`, `ClockChecks`) and gives `acq_time` on the server's clock (`correctClock: false` leaves it). Settings → Recording Format → *Check the clock while recording*, or `/v1/settings/time-check` (`enabled`, `server`, `interval_minutes`, `warn_ms`). A lab with its own time server on the local network — or PTP-disciplined machines — names it in `server` and gets sub-millisecond agreement between its computers; over the internet a check is good to a few milliseconds.
- **LSL accelerometer readings carry their own time**: the accelerometer stream beside an EEG stream is put on this computer's clock like the EEG (it used to be stamped with the moment each reading arrived). `ImuFrame` gained an optional `timestamp_s` for sources that stamp their readings.
- **Companion devices (setting, off by default)**: a second device recorded in parallel with every session, into the same recording and on the same clock — Settings → Recording Format → *Record an EmotiBit alongside*, or any connect targets through `POST /v1/settings/companion-devices` (`["emotibit"]`, `["emotibit:192.168.1.40"]`). The session stays its own device's: a companion's vitals, sensor streams and markers go to the `_vitals`, `_sensors` and `_markers` files, its motion and battery become sensor channels (`accel_x` … `battery_pct`), and its EEG / PPG / fNIRS frames are not recorded. A companion that is missing does not hold a session up and one that drops out does not end it: it is looked for again every five seconds, and every connection and loss is recorded as a marker (`emotibit connected` / `emotibit disconnected`, source `companion`).
- **Montages — by default and after the fact**: a recording's channels can be given labels, a kind each (EEG, EOG, ECG, EMG, reference, trigger, other), bad-channel marks, positions, a reference and a ground.
  - *By default*: Settings → Recording Format → *Montages…* keeps a library of named montages and makes one the default for sessions by device name (`EN32*`), device kind and channel count. Such a session records and analyses under the montage's labels; what the device called its channels is kept beside the recording.
  - *After the fact*: History → a session → *Montage* gives any recording a montage, replaces it or removes it. Nothing recorded is rewritten: the montage is a file of its own beside the recording (`exg_<ts>.montage`) and is laid over it when it is read; BIDS export writes its labels, channel types, bad channels, reference and ground.
  - *Written however is at hand*: labels in channel order (`Fp1 Fp2 F7 …`), single channels (`CH3=Cz`, `CH32=HEOG:eog`, `CH7=T7!` for a bad one), a JSON list, a `{recorded name or number: label}` map, a montage written out in full, or the name of a saved one. A montage may cover only some channels and may name channels a recording does not have; what does not fit comes back as warnings, not as an error.
  - *API*: `GET/POST /v1/settings/montages`, `POST /v1/settings/montages/save` (`{name, montage, defaultFor?}`), `…/delete`, `…/preview` (lay a montage over channels without saving), `GET /v1/history/sessions/montage?csvPath=`, `POST /v1/history/sessions/montage` (`{csvPath, montage | null}`).
- **LSL accelerometer**: the `<name>-Accelerometer` stream a source publishes beside `<name>-EEG` is recorded as IMU data (mm/s² converted to g).

### Performance

- **Smaller Parquet recordings**: raw EEG is written in row groups of 10 s of signal (sized from the stream's own rate, no longer one per 256 samples) with byte-stream-split encoding and ZSTD — about 2.6× smaller than before and 3× smaller than CSV on a 500 Hz test signal.
- **Band-metrics Parquet file 15× smaller**: `_metrics.parquet` was cut into a new row group on every flush — twice a second — and with twelve columns per channel (432 for 32 channels) nearly all of the file was per-column headers: about 700 MB an hour for a 32-channel stream. Row groups are now a minute of metrics, encoded with byte-stream-split and ZSTD and without per-column statistics. Values are unchanged.
- **Whole-frame recording path**: sources that deliver complete frames are written and queued for embedding without per-electrode buffering.

### Bugfixes

- **A recording that cannot be opened is retried**: when the recording could not be created (disk full, directory missing) the session used to run on while silently discarding every sample. It now raises an error toast and retries every 5 s until storage works.
- **Parquet crash recovery could lose every row group of a small stream**: the checkpoint that makes a row group recoverable was written before the writer's own buffer had been flushed, so a row group whose last pages were small (few channels, a low rate, well-compressing data) was not entirely in the file when the checkpoint vouched for it — after a crash recovery then fell back to the journal alone. The buffer is now flushed before the sync.
- **LSL time stamps drifted from the wall clock over long recordings**: the difference between the LSL clock (monotonic) and Unix time (disciplined against a time server) was measured once when a stream was opened. The two run at slightly different rates — in a one-hour recording markers ended up 54 ms from the time they were sent. The difference is now re-measured every 5 s and followed smoothly (at most 500 ppm), so samples stay evenly spaced while they and the markers keep to the wall clock.
- **Failed writes are counted**: CSV and Parquet write errors were ignored; they are now counted, logged and raise a toast.

### Dependencies

- **rlsl / rlsl-iroh 0.0.6 → 0.1.1** (from crates.io), which bring:
  - **A reader that fell behind lost its stream.** rlsl took its buffer length as 360 samples instead of 360 seconds and ended the feed of a consumer that fell further behind — 0.7 s at 500 Hz. A session that stalls for seconds (a slow disk, a long analysis step) now finds the whole stream waiting.
  - **Compressed LSL feeds.** The LSL adapter asks sources on other computers for a compressed feed (`stream-zstd`, about 70 % of the bytes on the network for an EEG stream); sources that cannot give one, and sources on this machine, send the plain protocol as before. `SKILL_LSL_COMPRESSION=1` / `0` forces it on or off.
  - **Streams that answered over IPv6 first.** Resolved LSL streams always carry the IPv4 address they answered from (an IPv6-first reply used to leave a stream unreachable), and inlets connect to that address first.
